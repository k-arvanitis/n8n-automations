# Buying Property in Greece — Step by Step

This guide explains how the purchase process works for both Greek residents and foreign buyers. The full process typically takes 8 to 12 weeks from accepted offer to registration.

## Step 1 — Property Search and Viewing

We shortlist properties that match your budget, preferred areas, and goals (primary home, holiday home, or investment). We arrange viewings, share full documentation, and give you an honest assessment of price versus the local market.

## Step 2 — Obtaining a Greek Tax Number (AFM)

Every buyer needs a Greek tax registration number, called an AFM. Foreign buyers can obtain one through the local tax office (DOY) or via a representative holding power of attorney. The AFM is required before any contract can be signed and before you can open a Greek bank account.

## Step 3 — Making an Offer

Once you choose a property, we submit your offer to the seller on your behalf and negotiate the price and terms. When both sides agree, the terms are recorded and a small reservation deposit is usually paid to take the property off the market.

## Step 4 — Appointing a Lawyer and Notary

A lawyer is strongly recommended (and effectively standard practice) for foreign buyers. The lawyer performs due diligence; the notary is a public official who drafts and certifies the final contract. The notary is mandatory for all property transfers in Greece.

## Step 5 — Title Search and Due Diligence

Your lawyer conducts a title search at the local Land Registry and Cadastre to confirm the seller is the legal owner, that there are no mortgages, liens, or claims on the property, and that the building is legal with no unauthorised constructions. This is the most important protective step in the process.

## Step 6 — Preliminary Contract and Deposit

For purchases that need time (for example, while a mortgage is arranged), a preliminary contract may be signed and a deposit of around 10 percent paid. This stage is optional; many cash purchases proceed straight to the final contract.

## Step 7 — Final Contract at the Notary

The notary reads the final purchase contract aloud and both parties sign. The buyer pays the balance of the price, and the property transfer tax must be settled before signing. Both parties (or their representatives with power of attorney) must be present.

## Step 8 — Registration

After signing, the contract is registered at the Land Registry and the Cadastre. Registration is what makes you the legally recognised owner. Your lawyer handles the submission, which usually completes within a few weeks.

## Typical Timeline

- Offer accepted to lawyer engaged: about 1 week
- Title search and due diligence: 2 to 4 weeks
- Mortgage arrangement (if needed): 4 to 8 weeks in parallel
- Final contract and payment: 1 day
- Registration: 1 to 3 weeks

Cash purchases with clean titles can complete in as little as 6 weeks. Purchases involving a mortgage or complex title issues take longer.

## Buying Remotely with Power of Attorney

You do not have to be in Greece for every step. Many of our foreign clients grant a limited power of attorney to their Greek lawyer, who then signs on their behalf. This allows the entire purchase to be completed without you travelling to Athens more than once, or in some cases at all.
